## 1. What E1/CSRDA-G proposed

S2R-COD trains a camouflaged-object-detection (COD) model on *synthetic* source data: LAKE-RED, a
diffusion inpainting model, takes a salient object cut out of HKU-IS and paints a fresh camouflaging
background behind it. A mean-teacher loop then adapts to real COD imagery.

Stage C proposed to make that generation **targeted instead of uniform**. The idea, in three steps:

1. Embed the real target images, cluster them, and find the clusters where the COD model is weakest.
2. Score weakness with **ES-disagreement** — how much the student and teacher networks disagree on
   an image. Disagreement is free to compute and needs no labels.
3. Spend a fixed generation budget **B** preferentially on the weak clusters, rather than spreading
   it uniformly.

The experiment to test it was a three-arm comparison at fixed compute: **arm A** adds nothing, **arm
B** adds B randomly-chosen synthetic images, **arm C** adds B ES-allocated ones.

### What "viable" would have meant

One thing only: **arm C beats arm B on Sα by a margin whose 95% confidence interval excludes zero.**
Not "the mechanism is sound", not "the signal correlates" — a measurable accuracy gain, C − B > 0,
distinguishable from run-to-run noise.

That is the bar this investigation failed to clear, and §4 explains why the failure is structural
rather than a matter of tuning.

> The original E1/CSRDA-G proposal document is not on disk. This description is reconstructed from
> `PRIOR_REVIEW.md` §0.3–§0.6 and the two audits. **UNVERIFIED** as a verbatim account of the
> proposal; it is our best reading of it.

### Statistical terms, one line each

| term | meaning |
|---|---|
| **Sα** (S-measure) | The paper's headline COD metric: structural similarity between prediction and ground truth, higher is better. |
| **MAE** | Mean absolute error per pixel, lower is better — a *calibration* measure, not a localisation one. |
| **IoU** | Intersection-over-union of predicted and true object masks — a *localisation/boundary* measure. |
| **Spearman ρ** | Rank correlation: +1 means two quantities order things identically, 0 means no relationship, −1 means reversed. Used instead of Pearson because we care about ranking clusters, not linear fit. |
| **Cohen's d** | Distance between two groups' means measured in pooled standard deviations. d=0.1 is a tenth of the spread — negligible; d=0.8 is conventionally "large"; d=4.7 is two distributions that barely touch. |
| **σ (noise floor)** | The standard deviation of the final metric across repeated identical training runs. It is the smallest difference the pipeline can distinguish from luck. |
| **2σ bar** | The rough threshold an effect must exceed to be credible against that noise. |
| **Statistical power** | The probability of detecting a real effect of a given size. 80% is the convention. |
| **MDE** | Minimum detectable effect: the smallest true difference a given number of runs can find at 80% power. |
| **AUC** | Area under the ROC curve: 1.0 = a classifier separates two sets perfectly, 0.5 = chance. |
| **TV distance** | Total variation between two distributions over clusters: 0 = identical occupancy, 1 = disjoint. |
| **Generative precision / recall** | Precision = what fraction of generated images fall inside the real data manifold (realism). Recall = what fraction of the real manifold the generated set covers (diversity). |
| **Permutation p-value** | Significance computed by reshuffling the data thousands of times and counting how often chance beats the observed result. Used where sample sizes are too small for the standard formula. |

## 2. The chain of questions

Each link is `claim we needed to check → how we checked it → what we found → what it implied`.

### Link 1 — Is the weakness signal real, or is it just measuring instability?

- **Claim to check.** ES-disagreement identifies images the model actually gets wrong, rather than
  images where two networks happen to be unstable.
- **How.** Compute ES exactly as the training loop does, then correlate per-cluster mean ES against
  per-cluster *true* error on labelled data (COD10K-test, CAMO-val). Repeat on four independently
  trained runs to rule out a single-run artifact. → **[B1]**
- **What we found.** ρ = **+0.82** against MAE per cluster (+0.86 at k=20, +0.88 at k=100),
  replicating at **+0.903 ± 0.041** across four independent runs, with cluster rankings agreeing
  ρ = 0.78–0.94 between any two runs. But against Sα — the headline metric — only **+0.40 to +0.65**.
- **Implied.** The signal is real, and this is the finding that survived. But it ranks clusters by
  *pixel-calibration* error, not by the structural error the paper reports. Allocating by ES means
  optimising a proxy correlated 0.40–0.65 with the actual objective.

### Link 2 — Should the allocation also correct for coverage?

- **Claim to check.** Adding a term that favours clusters the synthetic pool under-covers
  (`−λ_cov·log(n_s/n_t)`) should improve the allocation.
- **How.** Correlate the coverage term alone against true error, then measure what adding it does to
  the ES signal, at k ∈ {20, 50, 100} on both val and test. → **[B2]**
- **What we found.** ρ(coverage, true error) = **+0.006** on test — pure noise. On val it is
  **−0.717**, i.e. actively *anti*-predictive. Adding it at λ_cov=0.05 degrades ES from **+0.817 to
  +0.138** on test, and flips **+0.800 to −0.733** on val.
- **Implied.** Set **λ_cov = 0**. Not a compromise — the term destroys a valid signal. The reason is
  mechanical and provable without any labels: ρ(acceptance, n_s) = **+0.73 to +0.92**, so ranking by
  *missing* coverage is very nearly ranking by *infeasibility*. The clusters the term sends the
  budget to are precisely the ones the generator cannot reach.

### Link 3 — Can the generator actually be steered to a chosen cluster?

- **Claim to check.** Asking LAKE-RED for an image in cluster *c* yields an image in cluster *c*
  often enough to be affordable.
- **How.** Cluster the target images; for each cluster take the 40 nearest cutouts; look up where
  their real, already-generated LAKE-RED outputs land; accept if the landing cluster is the intended
  one. Compare against the rate at which unsteered generations land there by chance. → **[B3]**
- **What we found.** Under InceptionV3 features, **0.00%** — which is why an earlier review declared
  the approach dead. Under DINOv2 at native resolution, **26.67%** at k=20, and **20.62%** when
  measured on held-out generations. That is **12–16× above chance** (p ≈ 1e-112).
- **Implied.** Steering is genuinely real, and the earlier kill was an artifact of a weak embedder.
  This link *passed*. Targeting is feasible at roughly 20% acceptance and ~2.3 GPU-h per cycle-arm.

### Link 4 — Given that steering works, how different is the targeted data actually?

- **Claim to check.** The 1000 images arm C adds differ substantially from the 1000 arm B adds.
- **How.** Allocate B by temperature-softmax over cluster ES, take the nearest cutouts, and measure
  Cohen's d between that set and a uniform-random set in DINOv2 space — sweeping the temperature T
  over a 10× range. → **[C1]**
- **What we found.** **d ≈ 0.10** at every one of eight settings (0.084–0.099). The temperature knob
  — the one hyperparameter that defines "targeted" — changes the number of funded clusters from 1 to
  49 while barely moving d.
- **Implied.** This is where the approach dies. Steering works, but what it steers *toward* is
  almost indistinguishable from random. See §4(iv).

### Link 5 — Does more budget, or more compute, rescue it?

- **Claim to check.** Raising B, or the training loop's own dynamics, could amplify a small
  per-image difference into a measurable one.
- **How.** Read the actual sampling arithmetic out of the training code, and compute the pool-level
  shift as a function of B. → **[C2]**
- **What we found.** Two hard walls. (a) `total_step = min(len(source_loader), len(target_loader))`
  is pinned at 253 by the *target* loader, so every arm runs **9,867 identical gradient steps** —
  added data buys zero extra optimisation. (b) Arms B and C overlap by `B²/4447`, so the fraction of
  the added block that *differs* between them is `(1 − B/4447)`; the pool-level shift therefore
  peaks at B=2000 for a mere **1.26×** and falls to **exactly zero** at B=4447, where the two arms
  become the same set of images.
- **Implied.** No budget setting rescues the effect. This retracted an earlier "raise B" recommendation.

### Link 6 — How big would the effect have to be to see it at all?

- **Claim to check.** Whether the predicted effect clears the pipeline's own measurement noise.
- **How.** Recompute σ from six completed training runs' prediction files (no retraining), then
  translate d ≈ 0.10 into a predicted ΔSα using a response rate anchored on a known intervention. → **[C3]**
- **What we found.** σ(Sα) = **0.00356** per arm-run, so the 2σ bar is **0.00712**. The predicted
  arm-C effect is **0.000111 Sα = 0.031σ** — a factor of **64** short (51× at the optimal B=2000).
  Inverting: the added block would need **d = 7.09** to clear the bar, while the entire
  real-vs-synthetic gap is d = 4.70.
- **Implied.** Arm C would need to differ from arm B by 1.5× the whole real-vs-generated gap. That
  is not reachable inside the generator's output space. The verdict is robust to which σ we use:
  0.048σ at the smallest, 0.013σ at the CI upper bound — a null either way.

### Link 7 — What bounds any conclusion we draw?

- **Claim to check.** Whether pool composition or data leakage limits what the experiment can claim.
- **How.** Hash every foreground and every split pairwise. → **[D1]**, **[D2]**
- **What we found.** The foreground pool is exhausted — 4447 HKU-IS foregrounds, 4447 generated
  outputs, 4445 unique, so every addition is a *re-render of a foreground already present*. And
  `Dataset/Val/CAMO` is **250/250 byte-identical** to `Dataset/Test/CAMO`, so every checkpoint in
  every arm is selected on a test set.
- **Implied.** Two scope limits, stated rather than fixed: a null result is scoped to "targeting does
  not help *when the foreground pool is exhausted*", never to "targeting does not help"; and CAMO can
  never be reported as an endpoint.

## 3. Why this sinks the approach — the four load-bearing conclusions

### (i) The generator is near-unsteerable: the whole channel is 48 numbers

LAKE-RED decides what background to paint by looking at the foreground through a deliberately narrow
window. `LMP()` at `ddpm.py:1548-1564` runs SLIC superpixel segmentation over the foreground and
keeps only the **mean colour of each superpixel**. With `n_super_pix: 16`
(`config_LAKERED.yaml:72`), that is 16 regions × 3 colour channels = **48 scalars** — the complete
description of the foreground that reaches the background-generation path at `ddpm.py:1579`. → **[A1]**

**Measured three independent ways** (A1, all thresholds PASS): the config gives the token count (16);
the *trained weights* give the per-token width — `mlp_in.fc1.weight` is `(6, 3)`, so each superpixel
enters as exactly 3 numbers, independently of any config; and a live forward pass of the real module
with real checkpoint weights on a real sample yields `vec_fg` of shape **(1, 16, 3)**, 48 elements.

**And 48 is an upper bound.** `LMP` zero-fills superpixels that SLIC leaves empty
(`ddpm.py:1558-1560`), so the *effective* width on real samples averages **45.75** and ranges
**36–48** across 20 images. This is a new finding, not present in any source document: the channel is
at most 48 numbers wide and in practice narrower.

The cross-attention does retrieve from an **8192-entry** codebook (`bg_embed` is `(3, 8192)`) — but
those 8192 entries are *addressed* by only those ≤48 scalars. The bottleneck is the query, not the
codebook, and stating it any more broadly would overclaim.

Meanwhile **80.9%** of every output image is background the model invents from scratch; the
foreground occupies a mean of only **0.1913** of the pixels (median 0.178, sd 0.109, range
0.002–0.638 over all 4447 inpainting masks). → **[A2]**

In plain terms: we are trying to aim a process that paints 81% of the picture, using ≤48 average
colour values as the steering wheel. The colour lever is real but weak — the
foreground→background colour correlation flips from **−0.36** in real HKU-IS to **+0.45** in
generated output, but explains only about **20%** of the variance. → **[A3]**

*Precision matters here:* what is 48-dimensional is the **conditioning channel**, not the model's
capacity. The U-Net is unconstrained. The claim is that the foreground can only influence the
generated background through 48 numbers — which is exactly the pathway Stage C needs to exploit.

### (ii) The training loop adds zero gradient steps for added data

`MyTrain.py:306-307` sets `total_step = min(len(source_loader), len(target_loader))` and
`MyTrain.py:51` consumes the two loaders with `zip()`. The target loader is pinned at
`ceil(4040/16) = 253` batches. So whether the source pool holds 6824 images or 7824, **every arm
runs 253 × 39 = 9,867 identical gradient steps.** → **[C2]**

Adding 1000 images does not buy more optimisation. It *dilutes*: the probability that any given
source image is seen in an epoch drops from 0.593 to 0.517. This is why arm A is not a clean
control — it confounds "1000 fewer images" with "every image gets 13% more exposure" — and why only
B-vs-C is a valid contrast.

### (iii) The foreground pool is exhausted, so additions are re-renders

HKU-IS supplies **4447** foregrounds. LAKE-RED has already generated **4447** outputs, one per
foreground (4445 unique — two internal duplicates). There is no reserve of unused foregrounds. → **[D1]**

So "add 1000 targeted images" cannot mean "add 1000 new objects". It can only mean *re-render
foregrounds that are already in the pool* with fresh backgrounds. Arms B and C draw from the same
bank of the same objects; they differ only in which foregrounds, and which of K renders, they take.

### (iv) The targeted-vs-random difference is d ≈ 0.10, and no B rescues it

Arm C's added block differs from arm B's by **Cohen's d ≈ 0.10** — a tenth of a standard deviation —
and that number is invariant across a 10× sweep of the temperature that defines "targeted". → **[C1]**

Chain the three facts together and the arithmetic is unforgiving:

| step | value |
|---|---|
| block difference | d = 0.10 |
| block as a fraction of the pool | 1000 / 7824 = 12.8% |
| pool-level mean shift | 0.01278 SD |
| response rate (anchored on the MT→Ours intervention) | 0.00867 Sα per SD |
| **predicted ΔSα** | **0.000111 = 0.031σ** |
| bar to clear | 2σ = 0.00712 |
| **shortfall** | **64×** (51× at the optimal B = 2000) |

And **no budget setting helps**, because raising B makes the two arms converge on the same images:
the shift peaks at B=2000 for 1.26× and hits zero at B=4447. → **[C2]**

> The response rate assumes the pool-shift → Sα relationship is **linear**, from a **single** anchor
> point. This is stated as an assumption, not a measurement — it is the one genuine escape hatch. Two
> things narrow it: the anchor intervention added *real target-domain supervision*, which arm C does
> not, so the rate is if anything generous to arm C; and d = 0.10 is a **mean** shift, so
> higher-moment differences remain **UNVERIFIED** and are the only route by which the true effect
> could exceed prediction.

## 4. What survived as valid

This is not a takedown of everything. Three findings came through the adversarial audit intact, and
one of them is a transferable prescription.

**Uncertainty is a real weakness signal.** ES-disagreement predicts per-cluster true error at
**ρ ≈ +0.82** against MAE (+0.86 at k=20, +0.88 at k=100), and it *replicates*: measured on four
independently trained runs it gives **+0.903 ± 0.041**, with any two runs' cluster rankings agreeing
at ρ = 0.78–0.94. It survived a test-peeking attack too — on CAMO-val alone at k=15 it is **+0.967**
with a permutation p of **0.0002**, so it does not depend on test-set correlations. This is a usable,
label-free way to find where a model is weak. → **[B1]**

*With one scope limit, stated plainly:* ρ = 0.82 licenses "ES ranks clusters by pixel-calibration
error." It does **not** license "ES predicts where the model is bad" in Sα, which is only +0.40 to
+0.65. And the ES *scale* varies 40% between runs (0.0439–0.0612), so only the *ranking* transfers —
a fixed ES threshold would not.

**Targeting works.** Rejection sampling reaches **~20% acceptance** (20.62% held out, 26.67%
in-sample) in the deficient clusters at k=20 with native-resolution DINOv2 — **12–16× above chance**,
p ≈ 1e-112, and affordable at ~2.3 GPU-h per cycle-arm. The earlier "0% acceptance, this is dead"
conclusion was an artifact of a weak embedder and was wrong. → **[B3]**

**Dropping the coverage term was correct.** λ_cov = 0 is not a shrug; it is the measured result.
ρ(coverage, true error) = +0.006 on the well-powered split, and adding the term degrades a +0.817
signal to +0.138. It has a **label-free** justification too: ρ(acceptance, n_s) = +0.73…+0.92 means
coverage-ranking is inverse-ranking by feasibility, provable without any ground truth. → **[B2]**

**The transferable prescription:** *allocate by model disagreement, never by embedding-space coverage
counts — unless the generator's outputs are style-indistinguishable from the target domain.*

measured against the right baseline, generation is not merely *limited* but **destructive**: raw
HKU-IS has generative recall **0.745**, LAKE-RED output has **0.466** — a **37% relative loss** of
target-manifold coverage, bought for +0.042 precision. → **[A3]**

## 6. What this package does not establish

A fair account has to state its own limits.

- It does **not** show that generation-in-the-loop cannot work. It shows that *this* generator, at
  *this* conditioning width, with *this* exhausted foreground pool, inside *this* fixed-step training
  loop, cannot deliver a detectable Sα gain.
- A null is scoped to "targeting does not help when the foreground pool is exhausted". A new
  foreground source (CAMO/NC4K cutouts, another SOD dataset) would lift that limit — and change the
  paper.
- The effect-size translation rests on a **linearity assumption from one anchor point** (§3(iv)).
- Acceptance was measured on **pre-existing** generations, never inside a live loop with fresh
  sampling. In-loop acceptance is **UNVERIFIED**.
- A **fifth distinct seed** was never run, so σ from four distinct seeds carries a 95% CI spanning
  6.6× (narrowing to 3.9× with all six runs).
- Three claims about pool composition — |Ds| = 6824/7824/8824, "the pool is 30–35% real target
  imagery", "B=1000 is 18% of the synthetic part" — depended on iteration-2 pool directories that
  **no longer exist on disk**. They are carried as audit citations, marked
  **UNVERIFIED-NOT-REPRODUCIBLE**, and cannot be regenerated without re-running the CLS stage.

**The one experiment that would convert this diagnosis into a contribution has never been run:**
widen the conditioning channel — concatenate a target-cluster embedding into the cross-attention at
`ddpm.py:1579` — and show that acceptance *and* generative recall both rise. That is the difference
between a paper that describes a failure and one that prescribes a fix. It is real engineering on
someone else's diffusion codebase, and it is the honest next step.
