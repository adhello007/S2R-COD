% ABSTRACT -- written last

\begin{abstract}
Camouflaged object detection asks a model to outline objects that blend into their background.
Labels are expensive --- tracing a boundary you can barely see is slow --- so synthetic camouflaged
images are a common partial remedy. But which images should you generate?

We built and tested an answer: cluster the unlabelled real images the model will face, find the
clusters it is least sure about, generate images aimed there, retrain, repeat. The ingredients are
standard --- active learning, mean-teacher disagreement, clustering --- but the loop is ours.

It does not help. Across $36$ controlled training runs, aiming the budget by uncertainty does not
beat spending it at random; our design could detect an effect the size of the one reported for this
approach, but not one several times smaller, resolving $39\%$ to $45\%$ of it. More interesting is
why the idea looks like it works. Targeted selection does pick a visibly different training set from
a random one --- but so does any rule that concentrates the budget on a few clusters, including one that keeps our allocation's shape while destroying the uncertainty signal in it. The separation comes from
concentration, not targeting; the discriminating control is a same-shape shuffle.

Independently: $41$ of CHAMELEON's $76$ images ($53.9\%$) are re-encoded copies of COD10K and CAMO
training images. Exact hashing, the standard check, reports zero overlap because the copies were
re-saved in a different format. We claim non-independence rather than inflation, and release a
detector and a clean protocol. A control that returns zero is informative only if it could have
returned something else.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Camouflaged object detection asks for a pixel-accurate outline of an object that is hard to see on
purpose: an animal whose colouring matches the leaf litter it sits on, an insect shaped like a twig.
Its training masks are unusually expensive, because an annotator must decide where a boundary lies
when the point of the object is that its boundary is invisible. Labelled data is correspondingly
scarce, and one response is to manufacture more: image generators can place a real foreground object
into a freshly generated background and produce a plausible camouflaged scene. But generation
is not free, and every generated image has to be trained on. So if you can make a thousand images,
which thousand?

\paragraph{Our answer.} Let the model choose: train it, find the unlabelled real images it is least
sure about, aim the generation there, retrain, repeat, so each round of new data lands where the
model is weakest. We built such a loop and tested it. Every ingredient is standard --- uncertainty
sampling from active learning~\cite{settles2009active}, a mean-teacher disagreement score~\cite{tarvainen2017mean} as the uncertainty estimate,
clustering over self-supervised features~\cite{oquab2024dinov2,radford2021learning} so the budget spreads rather than piling onto
near-duplicates. The composition is ours, and it does not work.

\paragraph{What we found, and how far the measurement reaches.} Across $24$ training runs --- two
architectures, four conditions, three seeds --- a targeted allocation does not beat a random one:
the decisive gap is \textsc{within noise} on both architectures and both endpoints. The
qualification is that this campaign resolved coarsely: its threshold was $126\%$ of the improvement
the published approach reports, so it could not have detected that improvement had it been present.
A second campaign of $12$ runs tightens the threshold by $3.24\times$, and under \emph{that} bar the
decisive gap sits at $0.91\times$ threshold --- still within noise, marginally so. Both are therefore
\emph{no effect resolvable at this sensitivity}, never a demonstration of absence. A mean-teacher
positive control shows the instrument is not blind, detecting a real effect of $+0.0197$ at
$3.3\times$ its bar --- though not, as Section~\ref{sec:null} states, an effect the size of the
nulls.

\paragraph{Why it looks like it works, and a second finding.} Targeted selection really does produce
a training set measurably different from a random one --- but so does any rule that concentrates the
budget on a few clusters, including rules that keep our allocation's exact shape while destroying
the uncertainty signal inside it. What looked like the effect of aiming was the effect of
concentrating, so the discriminating control is not random selection but a same-shape shuffle.
Separately: the CHAMELEON benchmark is more than half training data, and the standard check returns
clean. Some of what follows is specific to the loop we built and some travels; we say which for
each. One pattern runs through both --- a control that returns zero is informative only if it could
have returned something else.

\paragraph{Contributions.} The first is independent of our method, the next three travel beyond our
setup, and the last is a property of the loop we built.

\begin{itemize}
\itemsep0.02em
\item $41$ of CHAMELEON's $76$ images ($53.9\%$) are re-encoded COD10K-train and CAMO training
images, while exact hashing returns $0$. Verified against an author-sourced release and calibrated
by a negative control that finds none where none should be. We claim non-independence rather than
inflation, and identify a published protocol whose unlabelled target pool holds $40$ of the $76$ test images
(Section~\ref{sec:contamination}).
\item The target distribution has no cluster structure to allocate over --- best silhouette
$0.1600$, no better in three embedding spaces (Section~\ref{sec:diss-structure}).
\item An uncertainty signal can contribute nothing beyond the concentration it induces: $+0.0073$ of
a Cohen's $d$ against its own shuffle, and with concentration held \emph{exactly} fixed, real,
destroyed and reversed targeting are indistinguishable in $12/12$ cells
(Section~\ref{sec:diss-signal}).
\item Uncertainty predicts pixel error better than structural error in six independent
signal--architecture tests, surviving an area control that clears its floor by no margin on one of
two covariates (Section~\ref{sec:diss-ordering}).
\item Why this loop could not have shown a gain: a pinned optimisation budget, a foreground supply
that is a bijection onto $4447$ copied objects, and a generated distribution narrower than its own
input; we also \emph{withdraw} one of our own candidate causes
(Section~\ref{sec:diss-instantiation}). None licenses the conclusion that closed-loop generation
cannot work.
\end{itemize}

\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}

\paragraph{Scope and closest systems.}
Camouflaged object detection (COD) requires pixel-level segmentation of objects whose colour and
texture closely match their surroundings; \citet{fan2023advances} provide a broad account of its
architectures, datasets, and evaluation conventions. This paper addresses a different question:
whether an \emph{acquisition policy} can improve a fixed synthetic-data pipeline. Synthetic
augmentation methods such as DatasetDM~\cite{wu2023datasetdm} address how to construct labelled
training data, whereas our loop asks which generated examples to add under a fixed budget.
The closest systems are LAKE-RED~\cite{zhao2024lakered}, whose background retrieval and
compositing we use unchanged, and S2R-COD~\cite{luo2025s2rcod}, whose LAKE-RED-based
synthetic-to-real self-training protocol we follow. Relative to these systems, the intervention
under test is the uncertainty-guided allocation layer: it ranks target clusters using
student--teacher disagreement and sends the resulting quotas to the generator. The comparison of
C10 with B therefore tests targeting against random allocation while keeping the generator and
budget fixed; the subsequent same-shape shuffle tests whether any difference is attributable to
the uncertainty direction rather than concentration (Section~\ref{sec:diss-signal}).

\paragraph{Acquisition and uncertainty.}
The design draws on active learning~\cite{settles2009active}, but its query is directed to a
synthetic generator rather than a human annotator. Mean-teacher consistency~\cite{tarvainen2017mean}
supplies the unlabelled disagreement signal, and $k$-means over fixed self-supervised features
implements a lightweight diversity constraint in the spirit of core-set selection
\cite{sener2018active}. Deep ensembles~\cite{lakshminarayanan2017simple}, MC-Dropout
\cite{gal2016dropout}, and BADGE~\cite{ash2020deep} represent alternative uncertainty or
uncertainty--diversity constructions, but are not evaluated here. The contribution is consequently
the controlled test of this composition, not a new detector, generator, or uncertainty estimator.
Benchmark definitions, architectural baselines, generative alternatives, and contamination and
reproducibility literature are summarized in Appendix~\ref{app:related}.

\paragraph{Evaluation context.}
We retain the standard COD setting summarized by \citet{fan2023advances}: COD10K and NC4K provide
the principal evaluation context, while CAMO and CHAMELEON are established complementary
benchmarks. SINet and SINet-v2 are used as fixed off-the-shelf detectors, and the reported
S-measure, E-measure, and weighted $F_\beta^w$ follow the conventions of the published synthetic-to-real
baseline. These choices make the experiment a test of allocation under the existing COD protocol,
rather than a comparison among detector architectures.

\section{Methods}
\label{sec:method}

The idea is easy to picture. Train a model on the labelled images you have; look at the unlabelled
real images it will be judged on and find the ones it handles least confidently; ask a generator for
new images resembling those, add them, and train again. What follows is our instantiation, specified
exactly, because a null is only as informative as the thing it tested.

\subsection{Experimental Setting and Closed-Loop Protocol}
\label{sec:method-setting}

The setting is source-to-real domain adaptation: we train on a \emph{source pool} of $4447$ labelled
image and mask pairs and want the model to work on a \emph{target pool} of $4040$ unlabelled real
images, with verdicts read from two held-out \emph{endpoints}, COD10K-test primary and NC4K
secondary. The target pool is not one distribution --- it mixes $3040$ COD10K images with $1000$ from
CAMO, separable by a held-out probe at up to $0.9225$ AUC --- so a method partitioning it is
partitioning a mixture (Section~\ref{sec:diss-structure}).

Training runs in two rounds. In round one a student trains on the source pool while a teacher --- a
slowly updated running average of the student's weights --- gives a second opinion on the unlabelled
images that the student must agree with. Between rounds, target images whose student--teacher edge
disagreement falls below a fixed fraction of the set average, meaning the two already agree, are
appended with the teacher's prediction as their label. Round two rebuilds both models from ImageNet
initialisation and trains on the enlarged pool; its best teacher checkpoint is evaluated. Two properties matter and neither is visible from ``closed loop'': the round count is a
fixed constant, not a convergence criterion, and the generator is never invoked from training but
renders its pool once, offline, the only returning edge carrying pseudo-labels rather than images.
It composites rather than synthesises
(Section~\ref{sec:diss-instantiation}).

\subsection{Uncertainty-Guided Data Allocation}
\label{sec:method-allocation}

Our allocation spends a fixed generation budget where the model is weakest, in four stages.

\textbf{(i) Group the target images}, so ``weak'' can name a region of the data rather than one
image: $k$-means into $k=75$ clusters over L2-normalised self-supervised embeddings, fitted once and
never refitted. \textbf{(ii) Score each image for uncertainty.} We need a number, computable without
labels, saying how unsure the model is: how far the student and its running average disagree. The
consistency loss already reduces that quantity, which is what makes it available on unlabelled
data:
\begin{equation}
\mathrm{ES}(x) \;=\; a \,\big\| \nabla \sigma(s(x)) - \nabla \sigma(t(x)) \big\|_{1}
\;+\; b \, \mathrm{BCE}\big( \sigma(s(x)),\, \sigma(t(x)) \big),
\label{eq:es}
\end{equation}
with student and teacher logits $s,t$, sigmoid $\sigma$, Sobel gradient magnitude $\nabla$, and
configured weights $a=0.9$, $b=0.3$; a cluster's score $\mathit{es}_j$ is the mean over its
members. \textbf{(iii) Convert scores into a budget split.} Budget $B$ is spread by a temperature
softmax over $\mathit{es}_j$ with $T = \alpha \cdot \mathrm{sd}(\mathit{es})$, at $\alpha = 1.0$ and
$B = 1000$; the temperature sets how sharply the budget piles onto the worst clusters.
\textbf{(iv) Fill each quota.} A funded cluster draws images by cosine similarity between its
centroid and a grey-masked cutout of each candidate foreground. Appendix~\ref{app:method} gives the
rest.

\subsection{Experimental Conditions}
\label{sec:method-arms}

Four \emph{arms} --- training conditions --- share one base pool and differ only in what is
appended. \textbf{A0}, the unpadded baseline, is the base pool of $4447$ pairs alone --- an
\emph{unclean} control, differing in pool size as well as content, and \emph{not} the configuration
corresponding to the published row, which is C10 (Section~\ref{sec:null}). \textbf{A2} adds the $1000$ original photographs of arm B's stems, separating pool size from
generated content. \textbf{B} adds $1000$ renders drawn uniformly at random, redrawn per seed;
\textbf{C10} adds the $1000$ chosen by the allocation above. A2, B and C10 all hold $5447$ pairs, and the claim this paper
turns on is the gap between C10 and B. Appendix~\ref{app:method} records what was held constant.

\subsection{Experimental Setup and Pre-Registered Decision Rules}
\label{sec:setup}

Every decision rule below was committed at a named commit before the runs it governs; amendments are
append-only and dated; thresholds that failed are reported failed, and two that \emph{passed} are
reported and then refused.

\paragraph{Endpoints and seeds.} The primary endpoint is COD10K-test $S_\alpha$ from the final
round's best teacher checkpoint; NC4K $S_\alpha$ is \emph{reported, never decides}, as are MAE,
$F_\beta^w$ and $E_\phi$. Seeds are $\{42,43,45\}$. CHAMELEON is not an endpoint, for a reason that is
itself a result of this paper (Section~\ref{sec:contamination}).

\paragraph{The decision rule.} Three runs of the same arm do not return the same score, and the size
of that seed-to-seed wobble is what any real difference has to beat. Let $\hat{\sigma}$ be that
wobble --- the pooled within-arm standard deviation of $S_\alpha$ across arms, at $8$ degrees of
freedom:
\begin{quote}
\begin{tabbing}
\textsc{real effect} \quad\= iff $\Delta > 2\hat{\sigma}$ \; and sign consistent $3/3$ seeds \\
\textsc{within noise} \> iff $|\Delta| \leq 2\hat{\sigma}$ \\
\textsc{real regression} \> iff $\Delta < -2\hat{\sigma}$ \; and sign consistent $3/3$ seeds \\
\textsc{inconclusive} \> iff $|\Delta| > 2\hat{\sigma}$ but sign not $3/3$ --- report as is, do
\emph{not} add seeds
\end{tabbing}
\end{quote}
So \textsc{within noise} means a gap smaller than the seed-to-seed spread, not a gap of zero. The rule is applied independently per architecture --- SINet~\cite{fan2020sinet} primary, SINet-v2~\cite{fan2022concealed} the robustness check,
neither overriding the other, a disagreement reported as a disagreement. At $n = 3$ we report no
$p$-value, bootstrap or multiple-comparison correction: verdicts are $\Delta$ against a
$2\hat{\sigma}$ bar with sign-consistency counts, and nothing converts them into significance
claims.

\paragraph{Two further arms that isolate direction from shape.} Arms C10 and B differ by
\emph{concentration} as well as by targeting, so that comparison alone cannot speak to the
uncertainty signal. A separate campaign of $12$ runs --- two architectures $\times$ two new arms
$\times$ three seeds, B and C10 re-scored rather than re-trained --- holds the allocation's shape
fixed and varies only direction. \textbf{CSHUF} permutes the per-cluster scores
across clusters, destroying targeting; \textbf{CINV} reverses their rank, making the allocation
maximally anti-targeted. CSHUF's permutation is the first of $64$ candidate draws with zero fixed points and
$|\rho| \leq 0.10$; its realised rank correlation with the true score vector is $-0.03351$, against
$-1.0$ for CINV. Because equality is the \emph{supporting} outcome here, set
distinctness was gated before any GPU time at a Jaccard bound of $0.50$: between $452$ and $511$ of
each pair's $1000$ injected images differ.

\paragraph{Provenance of the rules.} Appendix~\ref{app:method} gives the commit carrying each rule
and all three amendments, including one clause that no correct computation could satisfy.

\label{sec:null}

runs, $\Delta(\mathrm{C}-\mathrm{B})$ is \textsc{within noise} on both architectures and both

\paragraph{The honest limit: this campaign resolved coarsely.} A verdict of \textsc{within noise} is
only as strong as the noise, and here the noise was large enough that the bar a gap had to clear was
\emph{wider} than the effect the campaign was built to detect: $2\hat{\sigma} = 0.0179$ against this
repository's reproduction gap of $0.0142$, which it was declared able to half-resolve. At $126\%$ of
that effect, \textbf{this campaign could not have detected the reference effect had it been
present}, and the null survives only on the tighter bar imported from the falsification campaign.

That bar is $3.24\times$ tighter, $\hat{\sigma} = 0.0028$ against $0.0090$, pooled within-arm over
$\{\mathrm{B}, \mathrm{C10}, \mathrm{CSHUF}, \mathrm{CINV}\}$ at df $= 8$; it applies here because
that pool contains B and C10. \textbf{$\Delta(\mathrm{C10}-\mathrm{B}) = 0.0050$ against a bar of
$0.0055$ is $0.91\times$ threshold, $3/3$ sign-consistent: still \textsc{within noise}, and
marginally so.} The asymmetry matters: where equality is the \emph{supporting} outcome a tighter bar
is conservative, but here the claim and the null coincide, so the looser bar is anti-conservative.
Even the tighter one resolves only $39\%$ of the reference gap on SINet and $45\%$ on SINet-v2.

\paragraph{The instrument is not blind.} A null is only worth reading if the same measurement can
detect something real, so we checked that it can: we trained an arm we expected to be clearly worse
and asked whether this design would see the difference. It did. A mean-teacher arm --- SINet, three
seeds, base pool, single-round by construction --- reaches $0.6988$ on COD10K-test against C10's
$0.7185$: $\Delta = +0.0197$ against a bar of $0.0060$, or $3.3\times$ the bar, sign-consistent $3/3$,
verdict \textsc{detected}, the secondary endpoint agreeing.

This is a control on \emph{instrument sensitivity}, not an ablation --- the arm differs from C10 in
method, round count and pool size at once, so no component attribution follows and none is made.
\textbf{And it does not close the gap that matters most.} It shows effects of roughly the reference
magnitude are visible; it does \emph{not} show an effect the size of our nulls would be. The
control's own bar, $0.0060$, exceeds the decisive gap $\Delta(\mathrm{C10}-\mathrm{B}) = 0.0050$, so
an effect that size would not have cleared this pooling either. A positive control at $0.020$
licenses \emph{the instrument works}, not \emph{it would have seen the effect we are nulling}.

\paragraph{Two loose ends, neither of which flatters us.} Both of the campaign's largest effects sit just inside the bar: the decisive gap is $0.91\times$
threshold, and the largest falsification gap, $0.0050$ on \emph{NC4K}, is also $0.91\times$ its own
bar (on the primary endpoint the maxima are smaller, $0.0027$ and $0.0024$). Two \textsc{within
noise} verdicts at $91\%$ of threshold is not a comfortable null; a little more sensitivity would
flip one to \textsc{inconclusive}. And the ordering $\mathrm{A0}<\mathrm{A2}<\mathrm{B}\approx
\mathrm{C10}$ is monotone in all four cells, three steps sign-consistent $3/3$: if real, padding the
pool helps and targeting adds nothing. No step clears its bar, but we report it because it points
away from our conclusion.

\paragraph{Where these arms sit against the published numbers.} Our C10 measures
$S_\alpha = 0.7185$ against the $0.7136$ reported for the corresponding published configuration. A0
is the \emph{unpadded} baseline, not that configuration, and it passed its reproduction gate;
Appendix~\ref{app:anchor} gives that gate, A0's spread, and an untested pool-size mechanism for its
instability.

\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:dissection}

The null does not establish that the approach fails, only that no benefit is resolvable at this
sensitivity; it says nothing about the uncertainty signal, since C10 and B differ by concentration
as well as targeting; and its scope is inherited from the generator. Those three gaps are what this section measures. Some of what follows is a quirk of our setup and
some travels; Table~\ref{tab:causes} in Appendix~\ref{app:causes} assigns every cause.

\subsection{Concentration Rather Than Targeting}
\label{sec:diss-signal}

Suppose you aim a generation budget at the clusters where the model is least confident. The training
set you get really does look different from a randomly chosen one of the same size: train a probe to
tell the two apart and it succeeds easily, on images it has never seen. It is tempting to read that
as evidence that the targeting worked. It is not.

We re-ran the selection with the uncertainty scores shuffled between clusters, so the budget was
spread exactly as unevenly but pointed at the wrong places. The separation survived. We aimed at an
arbitrary cluster instead: it survived again. Only removing the unevenness itself, by drawing two
random sets of equal size, collapsed it (Figure~\ref{fig:thesis}a). The probe was detecting
concentration, not the uncertainty signal.

The numbers follow the picture. Targeted selection separates from a random set of equal size at a
held-out Cohen's $d$ --- how far apart two sets are in standard deviations, scored on data the probe
did not see --- of roughly $1.00$ to $1.23$, an order of magnitude above a previously claimed
$0.10$. Permuting scores across clusters gives $+0.9139$ to $+1.1403$ in the same cells, and an
\emph{arbitrary} cluster as much again. Paired across twenty cells the signal adds $+0.0073$ of a
$d$ against its own shuffle, winning $13/20$ ($P \approx 0.13$ under a fair coin) --- weak evidence
for a small positive contribution, not the coin flip we previously called it --- and is
\emph{negative} at $-0.0649$ against an arbitrary cluster, winning $4/20$ ($P \approx 0.006$). On the
same yardstick the strongest directional evidence points \emph{against} targeting. What targeting
buys is narrowness --- an effective-rank ratio of $0.634$ over $20/20$ cells, $0.53$ to $0.64$ in
the four peak cells --- covering no more of the target manifold.

Geometry is not performance, so we trained it too. The falsification arms hold concentration
\emph{exactly} fixed --- CSHUF permutes the per-cluster scores and CINV reverses their rank, both
reproducing the committed allocation's shape to five decimal places on all four recorded shape
statistics --- and vary only direction. All twelve cells are \textsc{within noise}, the largest gap
anywhere being $0.0050$ at $1.82\hat{\sigma}$. A directional pattern exists and we report it without
claiming it: C10 is best in none of the four cells, the anti-targeted CINV has the highest mean in
three of four, and the architectures disagree in sign on two of three gaps --- the signature of
noise. None of it reaches the bar, so it is not the verdict.

\textbf{Scope, and the trained half is the weaker one.} Negligible \emph{in this geometry} is
supported; zero and harmful are not. The trained comparison is attenuated three times over, and the
factors multiply: injected images are $1000$ of a $5447$-image pool ($18.4\%$), the pinned budget
dilutes exposure, and only $\approx 48\%$ of injected images differ between arms --- an effective
contrast near $8.8\%$ against a bar of $0.0055$. \textbf{So the $12/12$ trained result is weakly
informative, and the geometric result carries this claim.} That attenuation biases toward the null,
which is why we disclose it. \textbf{Transfers:} the control that matters is not random selection but a same-shape allocation
with the signal destroyed.

\subsection{Cluster Structure of the Target Distribution}
\label{sec:diss-structure}

Allocating cluster by cluster presumes the data falls into clusters. It barely does. The silhouette
score --- a standard measure of how cleanly points fall into groups, where values near zero mean
there are no real groups --- peaks at $0.1600$ at $k = 75$ in the space the pipeline uses, and the
other two spaces we tried reach only $0.1465$ and $0.0568$, all below any conventional threshold, so
the weakness is not an artifact of one encoder. The third fails more specifically: its silhouette
falls monotonically from $0.0568$ at $k=5$ to $0.0357$ at $k=150$, returning its smallest grid value
by hitting the boundary rather than finding a maximum. Our guard required an interior maximum, and
it failed. Two measurements reinforce this: an arbitrary preprocessing choice reassigns $5.4\%$ of
memberships, so an effect smaller than that cannot be attributed to targeting; and the pool is the
two-dataset mixture of Section~\ref{sec:method-setting}, separating from itself at up to $0.9225$.
\textbf{Transfers:} the check costs one silhouette sweep and should precede any method that allocates
over clusters.

\subsection{Uncertainty and Error-Type Ordering}
\label{sec:diss-ordering}

An uncertainty score is only useful if it points at the errors you care about. In camouflage that
means the boundary, which is what $S_\alpha$ rewards and what a per-pixel average like MAE largely
ignores. These signals track the per-pixel average better.

The ordering $\rho(\mathrm{MAE}) > \rho(1-S_\alpha) > \rho(1-\mathrm{IoU})$ holds in \textbf{six
independent signal--architecture tests}: three estimators --- student--teacher disagreement,
predictive entropy and ensemble disagreement --- crossed with two architectures. The table carries
eight rows because the ensemble is evaluated on two arms, but our own protocol treats the second as
a \emph{sensitivity check on} the first, so they are not independent and we do not count them twice.
The gap spans $+0.0708$ to $+0.3330$, all clearing the resolution floor of about $0.06$, the
smallest only just. The same criterion can fail, and elsewhere it does: aggregated over a boundary
band, only $2$ of $8$ rows pass and four go negative, outside our declared interpretation space,
which did not anticipate inversion.

\paragraph{The area control.} Our own explanation for the boundary collapse --- a whole-image mean is
uncertainty density times area --- \emph{applies to the whole-image rows too}: if MAE and the signals
both scale with area while $S_\alpha$ carries an object-size term that does not, the ordering could
be an artifact of the metrics. So we partialled area out, recomputing each correlation with size
held constant (Table~\ref{tab:areacontrol}).

\textbf{The evidence against the confound is the direction of the shift, not the pass count:
partialling object area \emph{raises} $\rho(1-S_\alpha)$ in all eight rows}, the opposite of what the
confound predicts --- area was suppressing the structural correlation, not inflating the pixel one.
The ordering then survives on $7/8$ and $6/8$ rows against a floor of $6/8$ declared before the run.
That floor is weak and we say so: $6$ of $8$ has $P \approx 0.145$ under a fair coin, so the
uncertain-area covariate clears a near-chance bar by zero margin. \textbf{Area accounts for part of
the gap but not its sign.}

\textbf{Scope:} correlational, one endpoint, and the \emph{magnitude} does not generalise ---
$\rho(1-S_\alpha)$ reaches $+0.5628$ on SINet-v2, so the claim is strictly ordinal.
\textbf{Transfers:} if you care about structural quality, uncertainty of these kinds is a better
proxy for pixel-average error than for what you want to improve.

\subsection{Setup-Specific Limitations}
\label{sec:diss-instantiation}

The reasons above would apply to anyone. Three more are ours alone, properties of the particular loop
and generator we used. The optimisation budget is pinned at $253$ and $127$ steps per epoch
regardless of pool size, so adding $1000$ images bought no extra optimisation, only a change of
mixture. The foreground supply is exhausted: the render set is a bijection onto the base set and not
one traced object shows any sign of regeneration, so the objects are copied rather than synthesised.
And the generated distribution is narrower than its own input --- real photographs of a different
genre cover the target manifold better than synthetic images generated from those very
photographs.

Each has an obvious rebuttal and each rebuttal is right: the first is a property of the training
schedule, not of synthetic data; the second follows from choosing a generator that composites by
design; the third is a distributional characterisation, so ``the distribution is far, therefore no
amount of it can help'' would be a non-sequitur. None licenses the conclusion that closed-loop
generation cannot work; they say only that this build could not have shown that it does. We also
\emph{withdraw} one of our own candidate causes, by source reading rather than measurement.
Appendix~\ref{app:tier1} gives every measurement, the withdrawn cause, and the evidence that the
coverage result alone does not depend on statistical power.

\subsection{Benchmark Contamination and Protocol Implications}
\label{sec:contamination}

CHAMELEON is one of the four benchmarks camouflage papers report on, and more than half of it is
training data. The usual check hashes the image files and looks for identical bytes; run it here and
it reports no overlap at all. It is not wrong --- it answers a different question. The copies were
re-saved in a different format, so not one byte matches while every pixel does. Nothing here depends
on whether Section~\ref{sec:method}'s method worked.

\paragraph{The finding, and the control that misses it.} $41$ of CHAMELEON's $76$ images ---
$53.9\%$ --- are the same photographs as images in the COD10K-train and CAMO training pool,
re-encoded, $40$ in the public COD10K-train split and one in CAMO. A model trained on COD10K-train
has already seen over half of CHAMELEON before being evaluated on it. We verified against an author-sourced release, byte-identical at $76/76$. Exact hashing returns $0$
collisions and is correct at that level: these are re-encodings, not byte-identical copies. The
number was right; the inference drawn from it was not --- and we hit the same trap ourselves, our
first implementation reporting $10/76$ ($13.2\%$) because contrast normalisation destroyed the
discriminative scale. CHAMELEON predates COD10K, so the parsimonious reading is that COD10K-train
absorbed these images, though only pool membership is measured.

\paragraph{The count is a property of the data, not of a tolerance.} Matches at mean absolute
difference $\leq 1,2,3,5,6$ are $11$, $26$, $37$, $40$ and $41$ of $76$: the count saturates. The
sorted nearest-neighbour distances independently place $41$ images below $5.51$ with the next at
$40.58$ --- a $7.36\times$ jump --- unchanged at shortlist depth $8$, $32$, or all. Two instruments
that owe each other nothing land on the same integer, and the other three endpoints show no jump.

\paragraph{A negative control, from the same instrument.} An audit that finds contamination wherever
it looks is worthless. The identical detector --- imported, not re-implemented --- returns a clean null on NC4K against the
COD10K test split: $0$ byte-identical, $0$ pixel-identical, $0/4121$ re-encoded at every tolerance
swept ($0$ of the $1082$ images actually checkable against it), and a minimum nearest distance of
$20.610$, $3.44\times$ the confirmation tolerance. NC4K's $1/4121$
in Table~\ref{tab:contamination} is a \emph{different axis}, the full training pool, against which
$1715$ of its images are checkable; the two must not be collapsed.

\paragraph{Non-independence, not inflation.} We expected to say how much a contaminated column
flatters a score, measured it, and found nothing: all eight difficulty percentiles for the leaked
subset land within $0.448$ to $0.559$, and the sign of the clean-minus-leaked difference
\emph{flips} with the mask release. The pre-declared reading --- a percentile inside $[0.25,0.75]$
means no skew --- is what stopped us writing the quotable sentence. Our claim is therefore
non-independence, not inflation: \emph{CHAMELEON is not an independent endpoint for COD10K-trained
models.}

\paragraph{The protocol: retire the benchmark, do not subset it.} Our released protocol originally recommended reporting CHAMELEON on its $35$ uncontaminated images.
We withdraw that: of $76$ images, $41$ are contaminated and $25$ more have no same-dimension
candidate and are \emph{unchecked, not clean}, leaving \textbf{$10$ verifiably clean}. A $10$-image endpoint is not an endpoint, and with two incompatible mask releases the honest
recommendation --- a change to one we already released --- is to retire CHAMELEON rather than subset
it. Report COD10K-test and NC4K instead, disclosing the unchecked share. The detector we release is checkable without our data, and
Appendix~\ref{app:masks} records its self-test. [TODO: author finalize ---
anonymised release URL for the detector, the contaminated-pair list, and the clean protocol.]

\paragraph{A concrete instance, from the protocol we test.} The method under test evaluates on
CHAMELEON, and its task setup states that for this family of benchmarks it \emph{``consistently use[s] the real
unlabeled COD10K training set as the target domain''}. It takes a precaution: when the test set is
CHAMELEON, those images are removed from the \emph{source} domain \emph{``to prevent target domain
data leakage''}. That closes the source path but not the target pool, since the case definitions adjust source
selection only --- and that pool holds $40$ of the $76$ CHAMELEON images, so a model following this
protocol self-trains on pseudo-labels over a set containing more than half the images it is then
tested on. We attribute no intent: the source-side removal shows the authors were alert to leakage,
and the target-side path is invisible without a re-encode-tolerant check.

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

We built a loop that aims a synthetic-data budget by the model's own uncertainty, and found no gain
we could resolve --- at $39\%$--$45\%$ of the improvement reported for this approach, with a positive
control confirming the design detects a real effect of $+0.0197$ but not one the size of the
null. The idea still seems reasonable; for it to have a chance you would need an optimisation budget that
grows with the pool, a foreground supply not already exhausted, and a generator wider than its own
input. Separately: CHAMELEON is not an independent test set for models trained on
COD10K, the usual check will not tell you so, and we release one that will. A control that returns $0$ is only as good as its
ability to return something else.

\section{Additional Related Work}
\label{app:related}

\paragraph{COD benchmarks and detectors.}
The standard COD evaluation suite comprises COD10K~\cite{fan2022concealed}, a large benchmark
with ten camouflage categories and pixel-accurate masks; CAMO~\cite{le2019anabranch}, which focuses
on man-made camouflage and occlusion; NC4K~\cite{lv2021simultaneously}, assembled from Flickr; and
CHAMELEON~\cite{skurowski2018animal}, an earlier unpublished collection that has become a de~facto
standard. The continued use of all four matters for Section~\ref{sec:contamination}: an image shared
by a training set and any reported test set contaminates evaluation for methods using the full suite.
SINet~\cite{fan2020sinet} decomposes COD into search and identification branches, while SINet-v2
\cite{fan2022concealed} adds a neighbour connection decoder for boundary refinement; we use both
off-the-shelf. SegMaR~\cite{jia2022segment} iteratively magnifies and re-processes ambiguous
regions, representing architectural progress outside our allocation experiment. We report the
structure-aware S-measure~\cite{fan2017structure}, enhanced-alignment E-measure
\cite{fan2018enhanced}, and weighted $F_\beta^w$~\cite{margolin2014evaluate} for comparability
with S2R-COD.

\paragraph{Synthetic generation and domain adaptation.}
Latent diffusion~\cite{rombach2022high} and DDIM~\cite{song2021denoising} provide the high-resolution
generator and deterministic sampling schedule inherited by our pipeline. Copy-paste augmentation
\cite{ghiasi2021simple} similarly composes object cut-outs with varied backgrounds; LAKE-RED applies
this compositional idea while retrieving background patches. CyCADA~\cite{hoffman2018cycada}, in
contrast, aligns source and target feature distributions with cycle-consistent adversarial training;
our loop does not operate at the feature level and instead feeds generator output into a
self-training objective. Other camouflage-image generators exist~\cite{chen2025realcamo}, but they
are outside the experiment, so the reported null is bounded to LAKE-RED.

\paragraph{Uncertainty-guided acquisition and self-training.}
FixMatch~\cite{sohn2020fixmatch} couples confidence thresholding with consistency regularisation;
our pseudo-labels likewise arise in an inter-round consistency step, but drive a second training
round rather than an online update. Pseudo-labelling~\cite{lee2013pseudo} supplies the hard-target
view of this step. DINOv2~\cite{oquab2024dinov2} and CLIP~\cite{radford2021learning} provide the
frozen representations used for clustering. These choices complement core-set selection
\cite{sener2018active}: the clusters spread the budget, while the disagreement scores determine
which clusters receive more of it. BADGE~\cite{ash2020deep} jointly uses uncertainty and diversity
in gradient-embedding space; our same-shape shuffle in Section~\ref{sec:diss-signal} is a direct
control for the corresponding entanglement in this allocation.

\paragraph{Benchmark integrity and negative results.}
Dataset-collection procedures can induce shared statistical bias~\cite{torralba2011unbiased}, and
near-duplicate analyses of CIFAR and ImageNet show that visually identical images can cross split
boundaries~\cite{barz2020train}. Related contamination concerns appear in language-model evaluation
\cite{wu2024antileak}; the mechanism differs here, but the need for explicit auditing is shared.
Our pre-registration follows reproducibility guidance~\cite{pineau2021improving} and the case for
publishing disconfirming results~\cite{karl2024position}. Finally, criticisms of learned comparison
measures under resizing and representation choices~\cite{parmar2022aliased,jayasumana2024rethinking}
motivate treating encoding-sensitive probes as diagnostics rather than as clean perceptual evidence.

\section{Detailed Method Specification}
\label{app:method}

\paragraph{Training.} SINet~\cite{fan2020sinet} at batch 16 for 40 epochs and SINet-v2~\cite{fan2022concealed} at batch 32 for 100 epochs, Adam at
$10^{-4}$, images at $352 \times 352$ with ImageNet normalisation. The EMA teacher uses momentum
$0.996$ under this task setting. The consistency weights in Equation~\ref{eq:es} are the configured
$a = 0.9$, $b = 0.3$ with the unweighted cross-entropy variant --- note that these are the values the
trainer sets for this task, not the loss class's own defaults. Strong views add random autocontrast
and Gaussian blur to the weak view. \texttt{cudnn.deterministic} is enabled and
\texttt{cudnn.benchmark} disabled; this reduces, and is not claimed to eliminate, kernel
nondeterminism.

\paragraph{What was held constant, verified.} Across all $24$ runs the base pool is asserted
byte-identical against a hash manifest with $0$ mismatches, both rounds read the unfiltered
$4040$-image target set, and every run shares one trainer invocation, checkpoint rule, endpoint set
and deterministic-kernel setting.

\paragraph{Optimisation budget.} Steps per epoch are set by the shorter of the source and target
loaders, which fixes them at $253$ (SINet) and $127$ (SINet-v2) in both rounds of every run regardless
of pool size. This is measured per run and asserted, and it is the mechanism behind
Section~\ref{sec:diss-instantiation}'s first cause.

\paragraph{Allocation sweeps.} Stage (iv) matches against a grey-masked cutout rather than a render
because at selection time the foreground exists and the render does not. The budget grid is $B \in \{250, 500, 1000, 2000, 3000, 4447\}$ and the
temperature grid over $\alpha$ is extended until both degenerate ends are reached, defined numerically
as concentrated when $\max_j p_j \geq 0.95$ and uniform when the total variation from uniform falls
below $0.01$, with caps at $\alpha \in [10^{-4}, 10^{3}]$. The campaign uses $\alpha = 1.0$, $B = 1000$.

\paragraph{Metrics.} $S_\alpha$ at $\alpha = 0.5$ is the primary endpoint metric; MAE is the
checkpoint-selection metric; $F_\beta^w$ and $E_\phi$ are computed and reported but decide nothing.
The $F$-measures apply $\beta$ in the non-squared form, so the metric name rather than a formula is
what identifies them.

\paragraph{The A2 control's one confound.} A2 pairs the authors' original photographs with the
authors' masks, whose mean foreground fraction differs from the raw masks by $0.0058$ on $1000$ of
$5447$ images. This affects $\Delta(\mathrm{B}-\mathrm{A2})$ and is disclosed; it does not touch
$\Delta(\mathrm{C10}-\mathrm{B})$, the decisive gap, since B and C10 share mask provenance, pool size
and render pool.

\paragraph{Pre-registration commits and amendments.} The four-arm rule is recorded at
\texttt{065dac6}, the commit carrying that campaign's first log block; the falsification campaign's at
\texttt{617a2e1}, before its first training run; the signal-generality check's at \texttt{6efde9f};
the area control's at \texttt{c2114af}; the positive control's at \texttt{c2114af}. Three amendments
exist, all append-only and dated. Two are reference-tolerance defects of the same kind: a frozen clause
requiring reproduction to better than $10^{-9}$ against a file recording six decimal places, and --- in
the area control, written by the same author who had just documented the first --- the identical
mistake against a file recording four. Both are replaced by exact agreement at the recorded precision,
which is the strictest satisfiable form, and neither altered a decision threshold. The third marks one
verification gate not applicable in scope after it was found to compare two architectures' signals.

\section{Setup-Specific Limitations and Diagnostics}
\label{app:tier1}

These causes are properties of this loop and this generator. They explain why this
attempt could not have demonstrated a gain; they do not license the conclusion that closed-loop
generation cannot work. Each carries a legitimate dismissal, which we state.

\emph{The optimisation budget is pinned} at $253$ and $127$ steps per epoch for the two
architectures, in both rounds of every run, regardless of pool size --- so adding $1000$ images to a
$4447$-image pool bought no additional optimisation, only a change of mixture. A reviewer may call
this a property of the training schedule rather than a result about synthetic data, and that reading
is correct: nothing here measures what happens when the optimisation budget grows with the data,
which is the largest open question this paper creates.

\emph{The foreground pool is exhausted; the background is not.} The render set is a bijection onto the
base foreground set --- all $4447$ renders trace into it, $0$ outside, $0$ base foregrounds unrendered
--- on the pool training reads as well as on ours, and the mapping is literal: interior error against
the source foreground is $5.603$ and $9.633$ mean absolute grey levels against background error near
$70.6$. Of the $8885$ objects with a valid interior comparison ($4443+4442$, since erosion leaves no
interior at all for four and five objects each under $1\%$ of frame), $0$ show any sign of
regeneration. This is an identity statement
about pixels, not a claim about similarity, which is what makes it resistant to the obvious
counter-argument. The dismissal --- this generator composites by design, so of course it adds no new
foregrounds --- is right, and what survives it is the scope statement every null here inherits:
evidence about targeting under an exhausted foreground supply, silent on whether a larger or more
diverse one would behave differently.

\emph{The generated distribution is narrow, and this evidence alone does not depend on power.} As
recall against the target manifold the synthetic pools reach $0.1277$ to $0.5421$ across three
embedding spaces, while the generator's own input pool --- real photographs of a different genre ---
reaches $0.7097$ to $0.7473$ and a different real camouflage dataset reaches $0.7921$ to $0.8995$: real
photographs of the wrong genre cover the target better than synthetic images generated from those very
photographs, a paired loss of $-23.6\%$ to $-82.9\%$. Effective rank falls $243.3$ to $193.2$ while
precision \emph{rises} $0.6490$ to $0.6886$ --- narrowing, not displacement. This is a distributional
characterisation, not a training-utility bound; reading it as ``the distribution is far, therefore no
amount of it can help'' would be a non-sequitur, and what generalises is the design, not the number.

\emph{One candidate cause we removed rather than kept.} A convenient explanation was available: that
foreground information reaches the regenerated background only through a narrow low-dimensional
summary. Examining the released generator's architecture refutes that account as stated --- four routes
carry foreground information into the regenerated region, the narrow summary is one, and the dominant
route bypasses it entirely. \emph{This is resolved by source reading, not by measurement:} no
experiment was run and no metric logged, and it withdraws one of our own candidate causes rather than
establishing a result. Showing the channel is not narrow does not show its capacity is used, so we
decline both the claim that the generator is not the binding constraint and that it is steerable.
