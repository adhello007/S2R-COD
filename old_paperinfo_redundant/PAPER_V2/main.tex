% ABSTRACT -- written last
% =====================================================================
\begin{abstract}
A control that returns zero is informative only if it could have returned something else. We report
two cases in camouflaged object detection where the default control certifies the comforting answer.
First, allocating a synthetic-data budget by model uncertainty appears to select a markedly different
training set than random selection --- but selection rules that keep the allocation's \emph{shape}
while destroying the uncertainty signal reproduce that separation entirely, and the signal itself adds
$+0.0073$ of a Cohen's $d$ over its own shuffle. In $24$ pre-registered training runs the targeted
budget does not beat the random one, and in $12$ further runs holding concentration exactly fixed,
real, destroyed and reversed targeting are indistinguishable in $12/12$ cells. Second, exact hashing
reports $0$ collisions between CHAMELEON and COD10K-train, while re-encode-tolerant matching finds
that $41$ of CHAMELEON's $76$ images ($53.9\%$) are the same photographs as training data; we claim
non-independence rather than inflation, and release a detector and clean protocol. We also find that
across four uncertainty-signal variants and two architectures, uncertainty predicts pixel error better
than structural error in $8/8$ pre-registered tests, and that this survives controlling for area. Both
nulls are bounded by sensitivity, which we quantify rather than assert.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Camouflaged object detection is label-scarce: annotating an object whose appearance statistics match
its surroundings is slow and expensive. A generative model that can synthesise camouflaged images
offers a way out, and once generation is cheap a natural question follows --- if the budget is finite,
where should it be spent? The answer the field's methods propose is appealing: partition the unlabelled
target distribution into clusters, score each by how much the model disagrees with its own moving
average, and allocate generation in proportion, so new data arrives where the model is weakest. Every
component is standard, each is cheap, and the composite is exactly the kind of closed-loop system that
ought to work.

\paragraph{The thesis: the default control certifies the comforting answer.} Testing that idea, we
twice found the standard instrument returning a clean zero where a better instrument does not.
Comparing a targeted allocation against a \emph{random} one is the obvious control, and it credits to
targeting an effect that concentration alone produces; the control that discriminates is a same-shape
allocation with the uncertainty signal destroyed. Checking an evaluation benchmark for training
contamination by \emph{exact hashing} is the obvious control, and it certifies as perfectly clean a
benchmark of which over half is training data; the control that discriminates is re-encode-tolerant
matching. In both cases the default returns $0$, the result is the one everybody would prefer, and it
is wrong. That pattern, not either result alone, is what this paper is about.

\paragraph{What we did, and the scope, in the same breath.} Across $24$ pre-registered training runs
--- two architectures, four arms, three seeds --- a targeted allocation does not beat a random one:
the decisive gap is \textsc{within noise} on both architectures and both endpoints under a rule fixed
before the first run. That campaign's bar is $126\%$ of the improvement the approach reports, so it
resolves coarsely; a second campaign of $12$ runs tightens it by $3.24\times$, and under \emph{that}
bar the decisive gap sits at $0.91\times$ threshold --- still within noise, and marginally so. Both are
therefore \emph{no effect resolvable at this sensitivity}, never a demonstration of absence. To show
the harness can resolve anything at all, we add a mean-teacher positive control.

\paragraph{Contributions.} Each maps to one section; the tiers say how far each travels.
\textbf{Tier 3} findings are independent of the method, \textbf{Tier 2} concern the target data or the
uncertainty signal, \textbf{Tier 1} are properties of this loop.

\begin{itemize}
\itemsep0.10em
\item \textbf{[Tier 3]} $41$ of CHAMELEON's $76$ images ($53.9\%$) are re-encoded COD10K-train and
CAMO training images, while exact hashing returns $0$. Verified against an author-sourced release and
calibrated by a negative control finding $0$ of $1082$ checkable NC4K images colliding with the COD10K
test split. We claim non-independence rather than inflation (Section~\ref{sec:contamination}).
\item \textbf{[Tier 2]} The target distribution has no cluster structure to allocate over --- best
silhouette $0.1600$, no better in three embedding spaces (Section~\ref{sec:diss-structure}).
\item \textbf{[Tier 2]} An uncertainty signal can contribute nothing beyond the concentration it
induces: $+0.0073$ of a Cohen's $d$ against its own shuffle, and with concentration held \emph{exactly}
fixed, real, destroyed and reversed targeting are indistinguishable in $12/12$ cells
(Section~\ref{sec:diss-signal}).
\item \textbf{[Tier 2]} Uncertainty predicts pixel error better than structural error across four
signal variants and two architectures, $8/8$ under a criterion fixed in advance, surviving a
pre-registered area control (Section~\ref{sec:diss-ordering}).
\item \textbf{[Tier 1, scoped]} Why this instantiation could not have shown a gain: a pinned
optimisation budget, a foreground supply that is a bijection onto $4447$ copied objects, and a
generated distribution narrower than its own input. We also \emph{withdraw} one of our own candidate
causes (Section~\ref{sec:diss-instantiation}). None licenses the conclusion that closed-loop
generation cannot work.
\end{itemize}

Decision rules were committed before the runs they govern, at named commits; amendments are
append-only and dated; thresholds that failed are reported failed, and two that \emph{passed} are
reported and then refused.

\section{The Approach Under Test}
\label{sec:method}

A negative result is only as informative as the specification of what was tested. We therefore give
the loop in full, and state which of its properties are pre-registered, which are inherited from the
released implementation, and which we measured rather than assumed.

\subsection{Setting and loop}
\label{sec:method-setting}

The task is source-to-real domain adaptation for camouflaged object detection. The labelled source
pool holds $4447$ image and ground-truth pairs; the unlabelled target pool holds $4040$ images and no
labels. The primary endpoint is the COD10K test split, the secondary NC4K. The target pool is not one
distribution: it mixes $3040$ images from COD10K with $1000$ from CAMO, and a held-out probe separates
those components at up to $0.9225$ AUC, so a method partitioning this pool is partitioning a mixture
--- a finding, not furniture, and Section~\ref{sec:diss-structure} gives the consequence.

Training runs in two rounds. Round one trains a student against the source pool while an
exponential-moving-average teacher supplies a consistency target on the unlabelled images. Between
rounds, target images whose student--teacher edge disagreement falls below a fixed fraction of the set
average are appended with the teacher's prediction as their label. Round two rebuilds both models from
ImageNet initialisation and trains on the enlarged pool; its best teacher checkpoint is evaluated.
Two properties matter and neither is visible from ``closed loop'': the round count is a fixed constant,
not a convergence criterion, and the generator is never invoked from training --- it renders its pool
once, offline, and the only edge returning from training to data carries pseudo-labels, not images. It
composites rather than synthesises, regenerating a background around a given foreground and copying
the original object pixels back (Section~\ref{sec:diss-instantiation}).

\subsection{Uncertainty-guided allocation}
\label{sec:method-allocation}

The idea under test spends a fixed generation budget where the model is currently weakest, in four
stages.

\textbf{(i) Clustering.} The target pool is partitioned by $k$-means into $k=75$ clusters over
L2-normalised self-supervised embeddings, fitted once and never refitted. \textbf{(ii) Deficiency
scoring.} Each image receives a scalar from the student--teacher disagreement --- the quantity the
consistency loss already optimises, computed without ground truth, which is what makes it available on
unlabelled data:
\begin{equation}
\mathrm{ES}(x) \;=\; a \,\big\| \nabla \sigma(s(x)) - \nabla \sigma(t(x)) \big\|_{1}
\;+\; b \, \mathrm{BCE}\big( \sigma(s(x)),\, \sigma(t(x)) \big),
\label{eq:es}
\end{equation}
with student and teacher logits $s,t$, sigmoid $\sigma$, Sobel gradient magnitude $\nabla$, and
configured weights $a=0.9$, $b=0.3$; the per-cluster score $\mathit{es}_j$ is the mean over its
members. \textbf{(iii) Softmax allocation.} Budget $B$ is spread by a temperature softmax over
$\mathit{es}_j$ with $T = \alpha \cdot \mathrm{sd}(\mathit{es})$, making $\alpha$ scale-free across
spaces and $k$; integer budgets follow by largest remainder, summing to $B$ exactly. We use
$\alpha = 1.0$, $B = 1000$. \textbf{(iv) Greedy foreground selection.} Each funded cluster draws images
by cosine similarity between its centroid and a grey-masked cutout of each candidate foreground --- the
cutout, because at selection time the foreground exists and the render does not.
Appendix~\ref{app:method} gives the remaining specification.

\subsection{Conditions}
\label{sec:method-arms}

Four arms share one base pool and differ only in what is appended. \textbf{A0}, the unpadded baseline,
is the base pool of $4447$ pairs alone --- the row comparable to published numbers, and an
\emph{unclean} control, differing in pool size as well as content. \textbf{A2} adds the $1000$ original
photographs of arm B's stems, separating pool size from generated content. \textbf{B} adds $1000$
renders drawn uniformly at random, redrawn per seed. \textbf{C10} adds the $1000$ renders chosen by the
allocation above. A2, B and C10 all hold $5447$ pairs, and the pre-registered claim is the gap between
C10 and B.

What is held constant is verified rather than assumed: across all $24$ runs the base pool is asserted
byte-identical against a hash manifest with $0$ mismatches, both rounds read the unfiltered
$4040$-image target set, and every run shares one trainer invocation, checkpoint rule, endpoint set and
deterministic-kernel setting. Section~\ref{sec:setup} adds two arms holding this allocation's shape
fixed while varying only the signal's direction.

\section{Experimental Setup and Pre-Registration}
\label{sec:setup}

Every decision rule in this paper was committed before the runs it governs, and we state them here,
before any result, because a null is only worth reading if the reader can tell it was not produced by
choosing how to look.

\paragraph{Endpoints and seeds.} The primary endpoint is COD10K-test $S_\alpha$ from the final
round's best teacher checkpoint. NC4K $S_\alpha$ is secondary and, in the pre-registration's words,
\emph{reported, never decides}; MAE, $F_\beta^w$ and $E_\phi$ decide nothing. Seeds are
$\{42,43,45\}$. CHAMELEON is not an endpoint, and the reason is itself a result of this paper
(Section~\ref{sec:contamination}).

\paragraph{The decision rule.} Let $\hat{\sigma}$ be the pooled within-arm standard deviation of
$S_\alpha$ across arms, at $8$ degrees of freedom. The rule, frozen before the first run:
\begin{quote}
\begin{tabbing}
\textsc{real effect} \quad\= iff $\Delta > 2\hat{\sigma}$ \; and sign consistent $3/3$ seeds \\
\textsc{within noise} \> iff $|\Delta| \leq 2\hat{\sigma}$ \\
\textsc{real regression} \> iff $\Delta < -2\hat{\sigma}$ \; and sign consistent $3/3$ seeds \\
\textsc{inconclusive} \> iff $|\Delta| > 2\hat{\sigma}$ but sign not $3/3$ --- report as is, do
\emph{not} add seeds
\end{tabbing}
\end{quote}
The rule is applied independently per architecture --- SINet primary, SINet-v2 the robustness check,
neither overriding the other, a disagreement reported as a disagreement. At $n = 3$ we report no
$p$-value, bootstrap or multiple-comparison correction: a design choice, not an omission. Verdicts are
$\Delta$ against a $2\hat{\sigma}$ bar with sign-consistency counts, and nothing converts them into
significance claims.

\paragraph{Two further arms that isolate direction from shape.} Arms C10 and B differ by
\emph{concentration} as well as by targeting, so that comparison alone cannot speak to the
uncertainty signal. A second, separately pre-registered campaign of $12$ runs --- two architectures
$\times$ three arms $\times$ three seeds --- holds the allocation's shape fixed and varies only the
signal's direction. \textbf{CSHUF} permutes the per-cluster scores across clusters, destroying
targeting; \textbf{CINV} reverses their rank, making the allocation maximally anti-targeted. CSHUF's
permutation is the first of $64$ pre-declared draws satisfying zero fixed points and
$|\rho| \leq 0.10$, chosen by a rule fixed before the draw rather than by inspecting its
consequences; its realised rank correlation with the true score vector is $-0.03351$, against $-1.0$
for CINV. Because equality between arms is the \emph{supporting} outcome here, two near-identical
arms would manufacture it, so set distinctness was gated before any GPU time at a Jaccard bound of
$0.50$: between $452$ and $511$ of each pair's $1000$ injected images differ.

\paragraph{Provenance of the rules.} Each rule is recorded at a named commit preceding the runs it
governs, and amendments are append-only and dated. The sharpest is a frozen clause requiring
reproduction of a committed metric table to better than $10^{-9}$, which no correct computation could
satisfy because the file records six decimal places. Appendix~\ref{app:method} gives the commits and
all amendments. It is not presented as minor: the author of that rule also authored its flaw.

\paragraph{The power statement, and the sensitivity analysis it demands.} This campaign resolved
more coarsely than it declared. Its bar, $2\hat{\sigma} = 0.0179$, is \emph{larger} than this
repository's own reproduction gap of $0.0142$ (mean teacher $0.7030$ to the full method $0.7172$) that
it was pre-registered as able to half-resolve --- $126\%$ of the effect it was built to detect. The
corresponding published gap is $0.0152$; we use the reproduction figure throughout because it is the
one measured on this stack.

The falsification campaign estimates a bar $3.24\times$ tighter, $\hat{\sigma} = 0.0028$ against
$0.0090$, and because its noise pool contains arms B and C10 --- the two arms of the decisive gap ---
that bar legitimately applies here. \textbf{Applying it: $\Delta(\mathrm{C10}-\mathrm{B}) = 0.0050$
against a bar of $0.0055$ is $0.91\times$ threshold, with $3/3$ sign consistency. Still \textsc{within
noise}, and marginally so.} We state the asymmetry rather than leave it to be found: where equality is
the \emph{supporting} outcome a tighter bar is conservative, but for this null the claim and the null
coincide, so the looser bar is anti-conservative. The conservative estimator was available where it
cost us nothing, and we have now applied it where it costs us something. Even so it resolves only
$39\%$ of the reference gap on SINet and $45\%$ on SINet-v2. Appendix~\ref{app:anchor} places these
arms against the published numbers, including A0's reproduction gate.

\paragraph{The aggregate pattern, which points away from our conclusion.} The ordering
$\mathrm{A0}<\mathrm{A2}<\mathrm{B}\approx\mathrm{C10}$ is monotone in all four cells, three of the
four steps sign-consistent $3/3$. If real it would mean padding the pool helps and targeting adds
nothing on top. The frozen rule does not convert it into a verdict --- no step clears its bar, and at
$n=3$ we pre-registered no test that aggregates across cells --- but our own standard forbids
suppressing a pattern for failing a threshold, and this one does not flatter us.

\paragraph{A threshold that passed, and was refused.} On the secondary endpoint,
$\Delta(\mathrm{A0} \rightarrow \mathrm{C10})$ for SINet is $+0.0109$ with sign consistent $3/3$,
against a bar of $0.0083$: the frozen rule returns \textsc{real effect}. We do not report it as
one, on two independent grounds each sufficient by itself. NC4K is the secondary endpoint and decides
nothing, and the comparison is against A0, which is both confounded in pool size and the least
stable arm in the campaign. We record it prominently \emph{because} it is the tempting one.

\section{What Generalizes: A Tiered Dissection}
\label{sec:dissection}

The null does not establish that the approach fails, only that no benefit is resolvable at this
sensitivity; it says nothing about the uncertainty signal, since C10 and B differ by concentration as
well as targeting; and its scope is inherited from the generator. Those three gaps are what this
section measures. We separate causes by scope: \textbf{Tier 2} findings concern the target data or the
uncertainty signal and travel beyond this instantiation, \textbf{Tier 1} findings are properties of
this loop and do not. Table~\ref{tab:causes} in Appendix~\ref{app:causes} assigns every cause.

\subsection{The target distribution has no cluster structure to allocate over}
\label{sec:diss-structure}

\textbf{[Tier 2]} Cluster-wise allocation presumes clusters; this pool barely has them. Its best
silhouette is $0.1600$ at $k = 75$ in the space the pipeline uses, and the other two spaces reach only
$0.1465$ and $0.0568$ --- all below any conventional threshold, so the weakness is not an artifact of
one encoder. The third fails more specifically: its silhouette falls monotonically from $0.0568$ at
$k=5$ to $0.0357$ at $k=150$, so the criterion returns its smallest grid value by hitting the boundary
rather than finding a maximum. Our pre-declared guard required an interior maximum; it failed, and we
record the failure rather than relax it. Two measurements reinforce this: an arbitrary preprocessing
choice reassigns $5.4\%$ of cluster memberships, so an effect smaller than that cannot be attributed
to targeting; and the pool is the two-dataset mixture of Section~\ref{sec:method-setting}, separating
from itself at up to $0.9225$. \textbf{Scope:} three embedding spaces and $k$-means only.
\textbf{Transfers:} this check is upstream of any signal question and costs one silhouette sweep.

\subsection{An uncertainty signal can contribute nothing beyond the concentration it induces}
\label{sec:diss-signal}

\textbf{[Tier 2]} Our most transferable result, and the first instance of this paper's thesis;
Figure~\ref{fig:thesis} carries it.

A targeted selection separates from a random one of equal size at a held-out Cohen's $d$ of roughly
$1.00$ to $1.23$, an order of magnitude above a previously claimed $0.10$. That separation is real and
is not caused by targeting: control arms keeping the allocation's \emph{shape} while destroying the
signal reproduce it --- permuting scores across clusters gives $+0.9139$ to $+1.1403$ in the same
cells, and an \emph{arbitrary} cluster as much again; only destroying concentration too collapses it. Paired across twenty cells the signal adds $+0.0073$
of a $d$ against its own shuffle, winning $13/20$ --- weak evidence for a small positive contribution
($P \approx 0.13$ under a fair coin), not the coin flip we previously called it --- and is
\emph{negative} at $-0.0649$ against an arbitrary cluster, winning $4/20$ ($P \approx 0.006$). Applying
the same yardstick both ways, the strongest directional evidence here points \emph{against} targeting.
What the targeted arm does is narrow: an effective-rank ratio of $0.634$ on average over $20/20$ cells,
$0.53$ to $0.64$ in the four peak cells, covering no more of the target manifold.

Geometry is not performance. The falsification arms hold concentration \emph{exactly} fixed --- CSHUF
permutes the per-cluster scores across clusters and CINV reverses their rank, both reproducing the
committed allocation's shape to five decimal places on all four recorded shape statistics --- and vary
only direction. All twelve cells are \textsc{within noise}; the largest gap anywhere is $0.0050$ at
$1.82\hat{\sigma}$. A directional pattern exists and we report it without claiming it: C10 is the best
arm in none of the four cells, the anti-targeted CINV has the highest mean in three of four, and the
architectures disagree in sign on two of three gaps --- the signature of noise. None of it reaches the
bar, so it is not the verdict.

\textbf{Scope, and the trained half is the weaker one.} Negligible \emph{in this geometry} is
supported; zero and harmful are not. The trained comparison is attenuated three times over and the
factors multiply: injected images are $1000$ of a $5447$-image pool ($18.4\%$); the optimisation budget
is pinned, so added data dilutes exposure; and only $\approx 48\%$ of injected images differ between
arms --- an effective contrast near $8.8\%$ of training content against a bar of $0.0055$. Stated
plainly: \textbf{the $12/12$ trained result is weakly informative, and the geometric result carries
this claim.} The attenuation biases toward the null, which is exactly why it must be disclosed.
\textbf{Transfers:} the control that matters for uncertainty-guided acquisition is not random
selection but a same-shape allocation with the signal destroyed.

\subsection{Uncertainty predicts pixel error better than structural error}
\label{sec:diss-ordering}

\textbf{[Tier 2]} Under a criterion fixed before measurement, the ordering
$\rho(\mathrm{MAE}) > \rho(1-S_\alpha) > \rho(1-\mathrm{IoU})$ holds on $8$ of $8$ whole-image rows ---
four signal variants across three families (student--teacher disagreement, predictive entropy, and
ensemble disagreement in two variants) crossed with two architectures --- seven of them at all ten
clustering seeds. The gap spans $+0.0708$ to $+0.3330$; all clear the pre-registered resolution floor
of about $0.06$, but the smallest only just does.

The same criterion demonstrably can fail, which is what makes its passing informative: aggregated over
a boundary band, only $2$ of $8$ rows pass and four go negative, outside our pre-registered
interpretation space, which did not anticipate inversion. We report it as it fell.

\paragraph{The area control, pre-registered after that failure and before it was run.} Our own
explanation for the boundary collapse --- a whole-image mean is uncertainty density times area ---
\emph{applies to the whole-image rows too}: if MAE and the signals both scale with area while
$S_\alpha$ carries an object-size term that does not, the ordering could be a property of the metrics.
We therefore partialled area out on the same partition and floors, with the consequence fixed in
advance, using two covariates: object area on the endpoint side and uncertain-region area on the
target side. The ordering survives $7/8$ and $6/8$ against a floor of $6/8$ declared before the run
--- \textbf{area-robust, with the second covariate clearing by exactly zero margin.} Three details cut
against the simple reading: partialling object area \emph{raises} $\rho(1-S_\alpha)$ in all eight rows
($+0.3433$ to $+0.4988$ for ES on SINet), the opposite of the confound's prediction; the single
object-area failure is a tie, crossing by $0.0012$; and both uncertain-area failures are on SINet-v2,
where $\rho(\mathrm{MAE})$ falls $+0.7070$ to $+0.4457$ once band area is removed.
\textbf{Area accounts for part of the gap but not its sign.}

\textbf{Scope:} correlational, one endpoint, and the \emph{magnitude} does not generalise ---
$\rho(1-S_\alpha)$ reaches $+0.5628$ on SINet-v2, so the claim is strictly ordinal and the boundary
question remains unreachable. \textbf{Transfers:} if you care about structural quality, an uncertainty
signal of these kinds is a better proxy for pixel-average error than for what you want to improve.

\subsection{Why this instantiation could not have shown a gain}
\label{sec:diss-instantiation}

\textbf{[Tier 1]} Three properties of \emph{this} loop and \emph{this} generator explain why the
campaign could not have demonstrated a gain, and none licenses the conclusion that closed-loop
generation cannot work. The optimisation budget is pinned at $253$ and $127$ steps per epoch in both
rounds of every run regardless of pool size, so adding $1000$ images bought no additional optimisation
--- only a change of mixture. The foreground supply is a bijection onto the base set: all $4447$
renders trace into it, $0$ outside, and of the $8885$ objects with a valid interior comparison
($4443+4442$ across the two pools, not $2 \times 4447$), $0$ show any sign of regeneration --- the
objects are copied, not synthesised. And the generated distribution is narrower than its own input:
real photographs of an entirely different genre cover the target manifold better than the synthetic
images generated from those very photographs, a paired loss of $-23.6\%$ to $-82.9\%$. That last
measurement is the only evidence here not bounded by statistical power.

Each carries a legitimate dismissal, which we state rather than await: the first is a property of the
training schedule, not a result about synthetic data; the second follows from choosing a generator
that composites by design; the third is a distributional characterisation, so ``the distribution is
far, therefore no amount of it can help'' would be a non-sequitur. We also \emph{withdraw} one of our
own candidate causes: the convenient explanation was a narrow conditioning channel, and reading the
generator's architecture refutes that as stated, since four routes carry foreground information and
the dominant one bypasses the summary. \textbf{This is resolved by source reading, not by
measurement}, and showing the channel is not narrow does not show its capacity is used --- so we
decline both the claim that the generator is not the binding constraint and that it is steerable.
Appendix~\ref{app:tier1} gives the full measurements.

\section{Benchmark Contamination and a Clean Protocol}
\label{sec:contamination}

This is the paper's thesis again, in its cleanest form: the default control returns $0$, the answer is
the one everybody would prefer, and it is wrong. Exact hashing is how dataset overlap is normally
checked; it reports the benchmark below as perfectly clean. It is not. Nothing here depends on whether
the method of Section~\ref{sec:method} worked.

\paragraph{The finding.} $41$ of CHAMELEON's $76$ images --- $53.9\%$ --- are the same photographs as
images in the COD10K-train and CAMO training pool, re-encoded; $40$ lie in the public COD10K-train
split and one in CAMO. A model trained on COD10K-train has therefore already seen over half of
CHAMELEON before being evaluated on it. We verified against an author-sourced release rather than our
own copy, byte-identical at $76/76$, so the result does not depend on which copy was audited.
CHAMELEON predates COD10K, so the parsimonious reading is that COD10K-train absorbed these images when
assembled --- but only pool membership is measured, and the direction is inference from chronology.

\paragraph{Exact hashing reports this set as perfectly clean.} It returns $0$ collisions between
CHAMELEON and the training pool, and is correct at that level: these are re-encodings, not
byte-identical copies. The number was right; the inference drawn from it was not. We hit the same trap
twice --- on prior work, and on our own first implementation, which reported $10/76$ ($13.2\%$),
roughly a quarter of the true count, because contrast normalisation destroyed the discriminative
scale. The first wrong number was ours.

\paragraph{The count is a property of the data, not of a tolerance.} Matches at mean absolute
difference $\leq 1,2,3,5,6$ are $11$, $26$, $37$, $40$ and $41$ of $76$: the count saturates.
Independently, the sorted nearest-neighbour distances place $41$ images below $5.51$ with the next at
$40.58$ --- a $7.36\times$ jump after the forty-first --- unchanged whether we verify the top $8$, the
top $32$, or every candidate. Two instruments that owe each other nothing land on the same integer,
and the other three endpoints show no discontinuity.

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

Instantiated in this loop with this generator, uncertainty-guided closed-loop generation yields no
accuracy gain resolvable at $39\%$ to $45\%$ of the improvement the approach reports --- a null bounded
by sensitivity and scoped to an exhausted foreground supply. What survives concerns the data and the
signal rather than our loop: the target distribution does not decompose into clusters any allocation
method could spend a budget over, the uncertainty signal contributes nothing beyond the concentration
it induces, and every uncertainty signal we could compute predicts pixel-average error better than the
structural error this task is scored on. A working system would need an optimisation
budget that grows with the data, genuinely new foregrounds, a boundary-aware signal and a steerable
generator. Both halves of this paper are then one lesson: a control that returns $0$ is only as good
as its ability to return something else, and twice the field's default cannot. Both return the
comforting answer; the remedy in each case was a control built to fail.

\section{Method specification}
\label{app:method}

\paragraph{Training.} SINet at batch 16 for 40 epochs and SINet-v2 at batch 32 for 100 epochs, Adam at
$10^{-4}$, images at $352 \times 352$ with ImageNet normalisation. The EMA teacher uses momentum
$0.996$ under this task setting. The consistency weights in Equation~\ref{eq:es} are the configured
$a = 0.9$, $b = 0.3$ with the unweighted cross-entropy variant --- note that these are the values the
trainer sets for this task, not the loss class's own defaults. Strong views add random autocontrast
and Gaussian blur to the weak view. \texttt{cudnn.deterministic} is enabled and
\texttt{cudnn.benchmark} disabled; this reduces, and is not claimed to eliminate, kernel
nondeterminism.

\paragraph{Optimisation budget.} Steps per epoch are set by the shorter of the source and target
loaders, which fixes them at $253$ (SINet) and $127$ (SINet-v2) in both rounds of every run regardless
of pool size. This is measured per run and asserted, and it is the mechanism behind
Section~\ref{sec:diss-instantiation}'s first cause.

\paragraph{Allocation sweeps.} The budget grid is $B \in \{250, 500, 1000, 2000, 3000, 4447\}$ and the
temperature grid over $\alpha$ is extended until both degenerate ends are reached, defined numerically
as concentrated when $\max_j p_j \geq 0.95$ and uniform when the total variation from uniform falls
below $0.01$, with caps at $\alpha \in [10^{-4}, 10^{3}]$. The campaign uses $\alpha = 1.0$, $B = 1000$.

\paragraph{Metrics.} $S_\alpha$ at $\alpha = 0.5$ is the primary endpoint metric; MAE is the
checkpoint-selection metric; $F_\beta^w$ and $E_\phi$ are computed and reported but decide nothing.
The $F$-measures apply $\beta$ in the non-squared form, so the metric name rather than a formula is
what identifies them.

\paragraph{The A2 control's one confound.} A2 pairs the authors' original photographs with the
authors' masks, whose mean foreground fraction differs from the raw masks by $0.0058$ on $1000$ of
$5447$ images. This affects $\Delta(\mathrm{B}-\mathrm{A2})$ and is disclosed; it does not touch
$\Delta(\mathrm{C10}-\mathrm{B})$, the decisive gap, since B and C10 share mask provenance, pool size
and render pool.

\paragraph{Pre-registration commits and amendments.} The four-arm rule is recorded at
\texttt{065dac6}, the commit carrying that campaign's first log block; the falsification campaign's at
\texttt{617a2e1}, before its first training run; the signal-generality check's at \texttt{6efde9f};
the area control's at \texttt{c2114af}; the positive control's at \texttt{c2114af}. Three amendments
exist, all append-only and dated. Two are reference-tolerance defects of the same kind: a frozen clause
requiring reproduction to better than $10^{-9}$ against a file recording six decimal places, and --- in
the area control, written by the same author who had just documented the first --- the identical
mistake against a file recording four. Both are replaced by exact agreement at the recorded precision,
which is the strictest satisfiable form, and neither altered a decision threshold. The third marks one
verification gate not applicable in scope after it was found to compare two architectures' signals.

\section{Tier 1 causes in full}
\label{app:tier1}

\textbf{[Tier 1]} These causes are properties of this loop and this generator. They explain why this
attempt could not have demonstrated a gain; they do not license the conclusion that closed-loop
generation cannot work. Each carries a legitimate dismissal, which we state.

\emph{The optimisation budget is pinned} at $253$ and $127$ steps per epoch for the two
architectures, in both rounds of every run, regardless of pool size --- so adding $1000$ images to a
$4447$-image pool bought no additional optimisation, only a change of mixture. A reviewer may call
this a property of the training schedule rather than a result about synthetic data, and that reading
is correct: nothing here measures what happens when the optimisation budget grows with the data,
which is the largest open question this paper creates.

\emph{The foreground pool is exhausted; the background is not.} The render set is a bijection onto the
base foreground set --- all $4447$ renders trace into it, $0$ outside, $0$ base foregrounds unrendered
--- on the pool training reads as well as on ours, and the mapping is literal: interior error against
the source foreground is $5.603$ and $9.633$ mean absolute grey levels against background error near
$70.6$, and of $8885$ traced objects $0$ show any sign of regeneration. This is an identity statement
about pixels, not a claim about similarity, which is what makes it resistant to the obvious
counter-argument. The dismissal --- this generator composites by design, so of course it adds no new
foregrounds --- is right, and what survives it is the scope statement every null here inherits:
evidence about targeting under an exhausted foreground supply, silent on whether a larger or more
diverse one would behave differently.

\emph{The generated distribution is narrow, and this evidence alone does not depend on power.} As
recall against the target manifold the synthetic pools reach $0.1277$ to $0.5421$ across three
embedding spaces, while the generator's own input pool --- real photographs of a different genre ---
reaches $0.7097$ to $0.7473$ and a different real camouflage dataset reaches $0.7921$ to $0.8995$: real
photographs of the wrong genre cover the target better than synthetic images generated from those very
photographs, a paired loss of $-23.6\%$ to $-82.9\%$. Effective rank falls $243.3$ to $193.2$ while
precision \emph{rises} $0.6490$ to $0.6886$ --- narrowing, not displacement. This is a distributional
characterisation, not a training-utility bound; reading it as ``the distribution is far, therefore no
amount of it can help'' would be a non-sequitur, and what generalises is the design, not the number.

\emph{One candidate cause we removed rather than kept.} A convenient explanation was available: that
foreground information reaches the regenerated background only through a narrow low-dimensional
summary. Examining the released generator's architecture refutes that account as stated --- four routes
carry foreground information into the regenerated region, the narrow summary is one, and the dominant
route bypasses it entirely. \emph{This is resolved by source reading, not by measurement:} no
experiment was run and no metric logged, and it withdraws one of our own candidate causes rather than
establishing a result. Showing the channel is not narrow does not show its capacity is used, so we
decline both the claim that the generator is not the binding constraint and that it is steerable.



\section{Contamination: corrections to our own work}

\paragraph{Corrections to our own work.} An author-sourced re-audit reproduced $8$ of $8$ of our
earlier measurements; its three mismatches correct our prior work, not the data. One declared
threshold is left \emph{failing} rather than reworded: we required every confirmed pair to differ in
its JPEG quantization table and compared with an inequality that scored a \emph{missing} table as a
differing one --- absence of evidence read as evidence --- because two files are PNGs carrying a
\texttt{.jpg} extension. Corrected, $41$ of $41$ pairs still carry independent re-encoding evidence,
$40$ by quantization table and one by container format. Separately, CHAMELEON was never a sanctioned
endpoint in the repository we built on; it was our own plan that promoted it, so the framing here is
not that we found a defect in someone else's evaluation.


\section{The endpoint-measured signal}

\paragraph{A correction to our own measurement.} Our first measurement correlated this signal \emph{on
the labelled endpoint} against endpoint error --- not the quantity the pipeline allocates by. Measured
on the signal actually available, correlation with pixel error falls by $+0.2100$ and $+0.2470$, from
$+0.8695$ and $+0.8754$ to $+0.6595$ and $+0.6284$. A signal validated where labels exist overstates
what is usable where they do not.


\section{The two CHAMELEON mask releases}

\paragraph{A second, unrelated reason the column is not comparable.} The two circulating CHAMELEON
mask releases are different annotations: opposite polarity, mean IoU $0.6932$ after alignment, only
$27$ of $76$ identical, and on identical predictions the choice of release alone moves MAE by
$2.7\times$. This has nothing to do with contamination, or with our method.
